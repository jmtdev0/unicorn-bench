How to find it

Three steps to the Unicorn

Monoceros straddles the celestial equator, so it can be seen from almost everywhere on Earth. Look for it on evenings from January to March.

  1. Find Orion

    On a winter evening in the Northern Hemisphere (a summer one in the Southern), look for the three bright stars in a row that make Orion's Belt.

  2. Draw the Winter Triangle

    Connect orange Betelgeuse on Orion's shoulder, Sirius, the brightest star in the night sky, and Procyon in the Little Dog.

  3. Look inside

    The Unicorn fills most of the triangle. Its brightest stars are only around fourth magnitude, so you will need a dark sky, patient eyes and a little imagination.

A modern constellation

Not in Ptolemy's list

Monoceros is not among the 48 constellations catalogued by Ptolemy in the 2nd century. It first appeared on a celestial globe by the cartographer Petrus Plancius around 1612, filling a dim gap beside Orion, and was later popularized by the astronomer Jakob Bartsch.

A unicorn black hole?

"The Unicorn"

In 2021 astronomers announced a possible small black hole, nicknamed "the Unicorn" both for its constellation and its rarity, orbiting the red giant V723 Monocerotis. Later studies have questioned whether it is a black hole at all. Elusive, as unicorns tend to be.
