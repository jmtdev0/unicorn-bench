The Unicorn Field Guide

Unicorns are shy, rare and almost always seen out of the corner of your eye. This guide teaches you where to look, what you might find and how to behave when one finally looks back.

Chapter one

Known species

Four kinds have been described with some confidence. Many more are rumoured.

🌲

Forest Unicorn

Moss-green mane, silver horn. Lives near old oaks and leaves no hoofprints on soft ground.

Seen
At dawn, in fog
Diet
Clover and silence
Rarity
★★★☆☆

Tide Unicorn

Gallops on wet sand at low tide. Its horn glows faintly blue, like a jellyfish.

Seen

Diet

Rarity
★★★★☆

Frost Unicorn

★★★★★

Chapter three

A short history of sightings

  1. c. 400 BCE

    Ctesias of Cnidus writes of swift one-horned "wild asses" in India, the first unicorn in written records.

  2. 77 CE

    Pliny the Elder describes the monoceros: a horse's body, an elephant's feet and a single black horn.

  3. c. 1500

    The Unicorn Tapestries are woven in the Low Countries, telling the hunt of a unicorn across seven panels.

  4. 1600s

    Apothecaries sell "alicorn" powder against poison; most of it is ground narwhal tusk.

  5. Last night

    Someone, somewhere saw a pale shape at the edge of a field and decided not to tell anyone. Yet.

Final chapter

The observer's oath

I will look gently, never chase, keep the place a secret, and believe what I saw even when nobody else does.
